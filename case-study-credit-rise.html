Case study

Credit Rise

An AI-assisted credit repair platform designed to turn a complicated, stressful credit-improvement process into a clearer sequence of review, prioritization, dispute tracking, education, and progress monitoring.

ClaudeLovableCursorGitHubFinancial UXAI-assisted development

The problem

Credit repair can force users to interpret information spread across multiple bureaus, negative accounts, dispute records, letters, timelines, and educational resources. The process can feel fragmented and difficult to prioritize, especially for someone who does not work with credit reports every day.

User pain points

  • Credit-report data can be difficult to understand at a glance.
  • Errors and disputes may need to be tracked across multiple bureaus.
  • Users need a clear sense of what has been found, what has been disputed, and what remains unresolved.
  • Negative accounts, utilization, and payoff decisions compete for attention.
  • Score-change estimates can be misunderstood if they are presented as guarantees.
  • Users may need education and document-preparation help without confusing the product with legal or financial advice.

The solution

I designed and built Credit Rise as a centralized dashboard that organizes the credit-improvement workflow around progress and next actions. The interface brings together score trends, three-bureau visibility, error discovery, dispute letters, dispute tracking, account analysis, payoff planning, score simulation, credit monitoring, and related tools in one navigation system.

Dashboard hierarchy

The main dashboard gives users immediate context before asking them to act. High-level cards surface the current score, errors found, resolved disputes, and an estimated change. The next layer shows score history and bureau-level scores, followed by negative-account detail and an illustrative scenario. This creates a progression from “Where am I?” to “What is affecting me?” to “What can I work on next?”

Turning complexity into navigation

The sidebar groups features by user intent instead of presenting one long list of utilities. Credit Scan sits in the overview area; Error Finder, Dispute Letters, and Dispute Tracker form a dispute workflow; Account Analysis, Payoff Planner, and Score Simulator support financial understanding and planning. That information architecture is intended to make a complicated process feel sequential rather than scattered.

Responsible score communication

The interface labels score changes and scenarios as estimates and explicitly states that outcomes are not guaranteed. The footer also frames the product as educational information and document-preparation assistance rather than legal, financial, or credit counseling. That distinction is important in a financial product because visual confidence should not become a promise of a specific outcome.

AI-assisted development workflow

I used Claude, Lovable, Cursor, and GitHub as complementary parts of the build process. Claude supported prompt-driven reasoning and product requirements, Lovable accelerated interface prototyping, Cursor supported implementation and iterative refinement, and GitHub provided version control and a durable development workflow.

What this demonstrates

Credit Rise demonstrates AI-assisted product development, dashboard UX, information architecture, responsible financial-product messaging, prompt-driven iteration, and the ability to use multiple AI development tools together rather than relying on a single model or one-shot generation.

Portfolio scope

This case study describes the interface, workflow, and product-design decisions shown in the build. It does not claim live credit-bureau integrations, guaranteed score increases, or specific financial outcomes beyond what is visibly presented as illustrative in the interface.
