Case study

Credit Rise

An AI-assisted credit education and dispute-management dashboard designed to make fragmented credit information easier to understand, organize, and act on without presenting estimated score changes as guarantees.

ClaudeLovableCursorGitHubAI-assisted developmentUX/UI

The problem

Credit information can be difficult to navigate because it is spread across multiple bureaus, specialty consumer-reporting agencies, negative accounts, dispute records, and financial factors. Users may know that something on a report needs attention without knowing what matters most, what stage a dispute is in, or how to organize the next step.

User pain points

  • Information is fragmented across Equifax, TransUnion, Experian, and specialty reporting sources.
  • Potential errors can be difficult to identify and prioritize.
  • Dispute letters and dispute status are easy to lose track of across separate tools or documents.
  • Negative accounts, utilization, and payoff decisions can feel disconnected from the larger picture.
  • Score simulators can create unrealistic expectations if estimates are presented like promises.
  • Credit-related products require a high level of trust, clarity, and responsible language.

The solution

I designed Credit Rise as a unified workspace that brings the major parts of the credit-improvement journey into one understandable interface. The dashboard combines bureau scores, error discovery, dispute preparation and tracking, account analysis, payoff planning, score scenarios, monitoring, and specialty-bureau coverage.

Information architecture built around the user’s questions

The navigation is organized around the questions a user is likely to have: What is on my report? What looks wrong? What have I disputed? What accounts are affecting me? What can I plan next? That structure reduces the need to understand the underlying credit-reporting ecosystem before the product becomes useful.

One dashboard, multiple reporting sources

The main dashboard presents an average score across the three major bureaus, individual bureau scores, a score trend, errors found, dispute progress, negative accounts, and specialty-bureau coverage. The goal is to turn scattered information into a single visual status view rather than force users to mentally combine separate reports.

Dispute workflow instead of isolated documents

Error Finder, Dispute Letters, and Dispute Tracker are treated as connected stages rather than separate features. A user can move from identifying a potential issue to preparing documentation and then tracking what has been resolved, which makes the product feel like a process instead of a collection of tools.

Planning without promising an outcome

The interface deliberately labels score changes as estimated and not guaranteed. The illustrative score scenario is framed as an example of what could happen if selected actions are completed and later reflected in reporting—not as a prediction or guaranteed result.

Trust and responsible UX

Credit Rise includes visible language explaining that the product provides educational information and document-preparation assistance rather than legal, financial, or credit-counseling advice. It also reminds users that credit outcomes are not guaranteed and that consumers may dispute credit-report errors directly with bureaus for free. These trust elements are part of the product experience, not an afterthought.

AI-assisted development process

I used Claude, Lovable, Cursor, and GitHub throughout the build—from product prompting and rapid interface iteration to implementation, refinement, and version control. The workflow allowed me to move from the initial product concept to a detailed multi-module interface while continuously testing whether the experience stayed understandable for the end user.

What this project demonstrates

Credit Rise demonstrates AI-assisted product development, prompt-driven iteration, UX architecture for complex information, dashboard design, trust-aware fintech UX, and the ability to turn a complicated real-world workflow into a structured user-facing product.

Portfolio note

The scores and score-change values visible in the interface are illustrative product data used to demonstrate the experience. They are not presented here as measured customer outcomes or guaranteed credit-score improvements.
